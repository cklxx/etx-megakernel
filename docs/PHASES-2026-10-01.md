# Phase by phase: our DSL tiles against vLLM's kernels, like-for-like

Round 24 put the two builds' phases side by side and one row — `qkv` — was read as a 3.29 µs/layer deficit that
would have justified rewriting the GEMV. Round 25 found the row compared our *folded* qkv against their *unfolded*
one; the real figure was 0.5–0.8 and our GEMV is the faster of the two. This document does the comparison once for
**every** phase, with the folds and the structural differences paired before anything is concluded, so the mistake
cannot repeat for the rows nobody checked.

**Sources.** Decode traces `results_2026-10-01/round24/t_r24_imp.log` (all imported vLLM kernels, 67994 tasks, 628
events, total span 6347.1 µs) and `t_r24_dsl.log` (today's best DSL build, 60362 tasks, 436 events, span 4804.1 µs),
both 32/32 tokens against HF greedy. Layer 11 is used throughout: it is mid-model, away from the prologue and the
`lm_head` tail. `delta` is this phase's first-ready to the next phase's first-ready — what the phase costs the layer,
including its exposed hand-off. Role-to-export mapping from `examples/vllm_llm/model.py`.

## The one phase where a kernel can be isolated

Layer 0 is the only layer the DSL build does not fold (`model.py`: `fold = ... (role == "qkv" and layer > 0)`), so
one trace contains both variants on the identical 5120×2048 shape:

| | tasks | body | layer-delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dsl` `in_norm_0_x` + `qkv_0`, unfolded | 8 + 256 | 4.05 + **6.29** = 10.34 | 5.9 + 9.6 = 15.5 |
| `imp` `in_norm_0_x` + `qkv_0`, unfolded | 8 + 304 | 3.10 + **7.14** = 10.24 | 4.1 + 11.5 = 15.6 |

Our GEMV is **6.29 against `wvsplitk`'s 7.14 at layer 0 and 6.75 at layer 11**, with 256 workgroups against 304.
Our norm replica is **4.05 against 3.10** — slower. Unfolded, the pair is a tie (10.34 against 10.24); folded at
layer 11 it is 10.04 against 9.25. So the 0.5–0.8 µs deficit on this row is our *norm*, not our GEMV.

## Conclusion 1 — on kernel speed the two tile sets are a tie; the whole 1.50 ms is structure

Taking only the rows where a kernel can be compared against a kernel doing the same work:

| comparison | Δ body µs/layer |
|---|---|
| GEMV, 5120×2048 (layer-0 control) | +0.46 |
| GEMV, o_proj 2048×4096 | +1.19 |
| MoE gate/up — ours also does silu | +1.87 |
| norm, 8-task x-form replica | −0.95 |
| top-k, 8-task x-form replica | −0.52 |
| MoE down — ours also applies the routing weight | −1.16 |
| **net** | **+0.89 µs/layer = 0.043 ms/token** |

**Our tiles are 0.04 ms/token faster than vLLM's kernels — inside the noise of this comparison.** The 1.50 ms the
DSL build actually wins by is structure the kernels dictate and we can change: `prep` against a one-workgroup chain
(+0.614 ms/token), attention placed by kv head with the *same* kernel (+0.355), and the folds that delete `silu_and_mul`,
`moe_sum_k8` and `post_norm` with their hand-offs (+0.500). That is 0.95 of the 1.50; the acquire accounts for most of
the rest, visible as waits around imported tasks (o_proj 42.78 against 24.49, reduce 35.98 against 15.78).

This is the quantified form of round 24's conclusion: **the DSL tiles earn their place by being editable, not by
being faster.**

## Conclusion 2 — what is genuinely ours to improve, and it is small

Ranked by the deficit against vLLM's counterpart:

| phase | deficit µs/layer | ms/token | caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| MoE down GEMV | 1.16 | 0.056 | ours also applies the routing weight, which theirs does in `moe_sum`; the trace cannot separate the two, so this overstates the gap |
| input norm (as folded into qkv) | 0.79 | 0.038 | the fold is still right — our *separate* norm phase costs more than the fold does |
| top-k | 0.52 | 0.025 | both 8-task x-form; a clean kernel-to-kernel deficit |
| **total if each reached its counterpart** | | **0.119** | |

The current gap to vLLM itself is 0.075 ms/token (4.937 against 4.862), so matching vLLM's kernels on every phase
would be *just* enough to pass it — with no margin, and these are the hardest rows left.

**Matching vLLM's kernels is not the ceiling.** `docs/BUDGET-2026-10-01.md` measures the residual against what each
phase's own bytes and width make achievable: MoE 0.476, `prep`+`reduce` 0.548, top-k 0.391 ms/token. Those are an
order of magnitude larger than the deficits above, which means vLLM's kernels are also far from the hardware limit.
The tile work worth doing is against the achievable rate, not against vLLM.

## What could not be sourced

- **The `qknr` build has no per-phase trace.** Round 24 kept traces for `imp` and `dsl` only, so every per-phase
  figure here is `imp`. The end-to-end headline "+1.13 ms" uses `qknr` (6.071), the better of the two imported
  builds; the per-phase table sums to `imp`'s 1.50. Both are stated rather than blended.
- **`moe_down` and `moe_up` cannot be split into GEMV and epilogue** from the trace: our tiles fuse the routing
  weight and the silu that the imported build runs as separate phases, so the body deltas mix kernel speed with the
  fold. The signs are reported; the magnitudes for those two rows are upper bounds on the deficit and lower bounds
  on the advantage.
- **`router`'s GEMV cannot be isolated** the way `qkv`'s was: the DSL build folds `post_norm` into router at every
  layer, including layer 0, so there is no unfolded control.
