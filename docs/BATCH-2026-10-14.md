# Batch 2 / 4 / 8 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite: design, rules and the deciding measurements (2026-10-14)

Written before any of its code (this file and `examples/dsv2lite/batch_floor.py` are the rule commit). No GPU has been
used for it. Labels: [M] measured, [E] estimate, [U] unknown.

The earlier two-request results (2026-09-27, docs/ETX_Technical_Design.md 15.12, examples/dsv2lite/results/2026-09-27*,
examples/vllm_llm/results/2026-09-27/vllm_ds_b*.json) are **context only**. They shaped what is prepared here: the
two-request graph exists, and vLLM's AITER setting mattered for this model. None of their numbers is quoted as a result,
used as a reference, or used to decide anything. Every number that decides comes from one new session on one box.

## 1. The question and the two quantities

For B = 2, 4 and 8 concurrent decode requests on one MI300X, bf16, context 1024, 32 greedy tokens per request: does ETX
serve them faster than the strongest vLLM configuration on the same box, in the same session?

Two quantities, reported and decided separately:

- **Total throughput** T(B) = B x 1000 / s(B) tokens/s, where s(B) is the time of one decode step that advances every
  request by one token. ETX: the median over the 32 measured steps of the host wall time of one step (launch to end,
  the host's `step_ms`). vLLM: the median over 5 samples of (t(32 tokens) - t(1 token)) / 31 for the whole batch, the
  method every vLLM number in this repository uses (examples/llm/vllm_bench.py).
- **Per-request latency** l(B): for each request, the median over tokens 1..31 of the time between the completion of its
  token t-1 and its token t, measured on the device clock from that request's own timestamps (host.hip `token_time`);
  l(B) is the worst request's value. vLLM's offline API has no per-request clock; all its sequences advance together
  each step, so its per-request latency is its step time. That is stated next to every vLLM latency.

With one launch per step, ETX's per-request latency is close to its step time too. The two quantities are still kept
apart: they come from different clocks, and a design that does not advance every request every step would separate
them. Neither is derived from the other in any report.

Reported next to each verdict, without a rule: ETX's throughput scaling T_E(B) / T_E(1) and latency inflation
l_E(B) / l_E(1) against the kept batch-1 arm of the same round, and vLLM's own scaling.

## 2. Ceiling first (examples/dsv2lite/batch_floor.py, [E])

Bytes of one step and the floor at 4.3 TB/s. "Replicated" is ETX today (every request reads every weight).
"Batched" reads every weight once for all B columns: dense weights, shared experts and lm_head once, routed experts
once per distinct expert (uniform routing: E(B) = 64 (1 - (58/64)^B)), the latent KV cache once per request.

| B | distinct experts / layer [E] | replicated GB | replicated floor | batched GB | batched floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.00 | 4.94 | 1.15 ms | 4.94 | 1.15 ms |
| 2 | 11.44 | 9.87 | 2.30 ms | 7.41 | 1.72 ms |
| 4 | 20.83 | 19.74 | 4.59 ms | 11.70 | 2.72 ms |
| 8 | 34.88 | 39.48 | 9.18 ms | 18.15 | 4.22 ms |

What this says before any measurement:
- At B = 8 replication cannot finish a step in less than 9.18 ms, against 4.22 ms for any system that reads each weight
  once. If vLLM's B = 8 step on the box is below 9.18 ms, replicated ETX loses at B = 8 by physics, whatever its schedule.
- At B = 2 the two floors are 2.30 and 1.72 ms. ETX's batch-1 token takes about 3.2 ms of device time (kept arm, 2026-10-13 hour [M]),
  far above both: at small B the step is latency-bound, which is where a megakernel can still win.
- The expert union is the largest input to the batched floor. The session measures it on its own prompts (section 6).

## 3. What runs (arms)

**ETX** (examples/dsv2lite/arms.py; every build compiled on the box and checked locally with ROCm 7.2.4 first):

| arm | B | build | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| `alld4r0l1` | 1 | the kept arm, unchanged | today's best single request |
| `x2` | 2 | five levers + `l1_loads`, coherence l1, no D4; `ETX_DSV2_REQUESTS=2`, coupling none, `-DETX_DS_REQUESTS=2 -DETX_SLOT_BY_CU` | two workgroups per CU, one per request (the existing two-request graph) |
| `x4` | 4 | same recipe, 4 requests | request r on workgroup slot r mod 2; on a slot, requests take turns layer by layer |
| `x8` | 8 | same recipe, 8 requests | as x4, four requests per slot |

- **Why no D4 in x arms:** D4 refuses multiple requests (graph.py), and lifting that is a separate change with its own
  risk. K1 and M1 are not in the x arms either; they are still unmeasured on the kept arm.
- **Why the x arms fit:** a local ROCm 7.2.4 compile of the x2 recipe gives 248 VGPR + 6 AGPR, 0 scratch, 2 waves per
  SIMD (`rocm_build_check.sh`, this branch, 2026-10-14) [M, compile]. Two workgroups per CU is the residency limit at
  this register count, so x4 and x8 cannot get more workgroups. Their extra requests share the two slots.
- **Why x4 / x8 are safe to queue that way:** every grid of request r, layer L is created before any grid of request
  r + 2, layer L, and every in-body wait (fleet's PUB_WAIT, D4-free here) targets a grid created earlier. Each static
  queue keeps creation order, so the earliest unfinished task is always runnable: no deadlock. Fleet's LDS hand-offs
  (gate_up -> down routing) stay adjacent on their worker. Both properties are checked on the lowered plan by tests.
- **Prediction, written now [E]:** x2 reaches 1.2-1.5x the kept arm's throughput. x4 and x8 stay within +-25% of x2's
  throughput, because the extra requests serialize per slot. Expected verdicts: B = 2 TIE or LOSS, B = 4 and 8 LOSS.
  These arms give ETX's honest numbers today and test per-request correctness at B = 8. The build decision they feed
  is in section 5.

**vLLM** (`vllm/vllm-openai-rocm@sha256:bb44b39a...`, the image of the Qwen comparisons; DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite-Base
HF weights, the same download the ETX weights are packed from; bf16):
- Two configurations: `default` (`VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER=0`) and `aiter` (`=1`).
- One engine per process measures B = 1, 2, 4 and 8 in turn (prompts 0..B-1), 5 samples each after a warm-up.

## 4. Correctness gates (per request)

**Prompts and goldens.** Request r's prompt is fleet's 1024-token golden prompt rotated by 97 r tokens, the
construction `vllm_bench.py --batch` and `reference_rotated.py` already use. For r = 1..7 the box runs fleet's HF
reference for that prompt (`reference_rotated.py --rotate 97r`). Each run writes golden.npz (prompt ids + 33 greedy
ids), fleet_cache.bin, golden_tokens.txt and golden_hidden.bin into `$ETX_DSV2_DATA/r<r>/`. Request 0 is the pinned,
preflight-checked set. The prep job refuses the set if any two prompts or any two golden token sequences are equal. It
records the sha256 of every per-request file.

**ETX.** An invocation counts only if all of these hold:
- the run decoded exactly B requests;
- every request has 32/32 free-greedy tokens equal to its own golden, 27/27 consecutive layers within the hidden-state
  gate (its own golden_hidden.bin), and a 32/32 warm-up;
- no MISMATCH or FAIL line appears;
- the host refused nothing (it refuses identical goldens between requests).

One failing request fails the invocation. An arm with a failed invocation is NOT COMPLETE at its B: no number, no
verdict. Before the rounds, a one-invocation gate per x arm decides whether the arm enters them. A failing arm is
reported NOT COMPLETE, and the session goes on without it.

**vLLM.** A configuration at batch B is legal only if every sequence generates exactly 32 tokens equal to its golden's
first 32 ids, on the warm-up and on every timed sample. The baseline at B is the faster legal configuration on its
selection-round median. If neither configuration is legal at B, the verdict at B is NO LEGAL BASELINE: timings are
reported, no claim is made. This is the rule every vLLM reference in the repository follows (32/32 arms only).

## 5. Rules (pre-registered) and what happens next

**Rounds.** Round 0 selects the vLLM baseline (both configurations, one process each). Rounds 1-5 each run, in this
order: `alld4r0l1`, `x2`, `x4`, `x8`, then the selected vLLM configuration(s). The order is reversed on even rounds.
Each round gives one pair per B: ETX arm at B against vLLM's baseline at B. Every invocation is a fresh process.

**Verdict, per B and per quantity**, from the five pairs (gains as exact rationals of integer nanoseconds):
- throughput gain of pair k: g_k = s_V,k / s_E,k - 1 (positive: ETX serves more tokens per second);
- latency gain of pair k: h_k = l_V,k / l_E,k - 1 (positive: ETX's worst request waits less per token);
- **WIN** if all five gains > 0, median >= 2% and median > 2 x spread;
- **LOSS** if all five gains < 0, |median| >= 2% and |median| > 2 x spread;
- **TIE** otherwise: no claim.

This is the repository's claim rule (2%, every pair, twice the spread), applied to each quantity on its own. B = 1 gets
the same rule as a reference point; it is not a batch claim.

**What happens next, decided by throughput** (the purpose of batching). Latency verdicts are reported, not acted on.
- **WIN at B:** replication is ETX's mode at B. The result goes in the gains ledger. The Qwen batch study (section 7)
  follows.
- **LOSS or TIE at B = 4 or 8:** the column-batched decision. With the session's own measurements, compute
  P(B) = s_E(1) + (batched bytes(B) - bytes(1)) / 3.5 TB/s. Here s_E(1) is the kept arm's median step, and the bytes use
  the measured expert union (section 6). This is the optimistic projection: batch 1's critical path, plus the extra
  bytes streamed at a near-peak rate.
  - If P(B) <= 0.9 x s_V(B) for B = 4 or B = 8: design and build column-batched fleet tiles (stage B2 below), with
    their own threshold written before their code.
  - Otherwise: ETX does not pursue throughput at that B. vLLM is within 10% of the best a batched ETX could plausibly
    do there, and that result is written down.
- **LOSS or TIE at B = 2 only:** no build decision follows from B = 2 alone. Its trace is the next diagnostic.
- **NOT COMPLETE or NO LEGAL BASELINE:** reported as such. The next session reruns that B only, after a local fix.

**Stage B2 (designed, not built):** column-batched tiles read each weight once for the B activation columns.
- First the request-independent phases: qkv, W_UK, W_UV, o_proj, the router, the shared experts, the dense layer and
  lm_head.
- Then the routed experts, grouped by distinct expert.
- Attention stays per request.

The B = 8 floor goes from 9.18 to 4.22 ms [E]. This is new tile code in fleet's bodies, so it needs the measurement
above first.

## 6. The session (examples/sessions/ds_batch.py)

One MI300X, GPU 0. No provisioning without the owner's approval.

**Preparation:** the ds_inplace prep chain (environment, pinned fleet export, model download, request 0's reference,
packed weights, preflight). Then:
- `prep_goldens` (GPU): the 7 rotated references and their checks;
- `vllm_goldens` (CPU): each request's prompt and golden ids for vLLM;
- `docker_pull` (CPU, in parallel): the pinned image;
- the builds (CPU, in parallel): x2, x4, x8 and the kept arm.

**GPU lane, strictly in order:**
1. `bgate_x2`, `bgate_x4`, `bgate_x8`: one gated invocation each. Correctness first; a failure removes only that arm.
2. `vsel`: vLLM round 0, both configurations, B = 1, 2, 4, 8.
3. `brounds`: rounds 1-5 (section 5).
4. `routing`: HF routing on the 8 prompts, mean distinct experts per layer for 1, 2, 4 and 8 sequences (the input to
   P(B)). Measurement only, after every timing.
5. `breport`: verdicts, scalings, P(B), and the next step that section 5 assigns.

**Budget [E]:**

| part | estimate | basis |
|---|---|---|
| Prep to preflight | 4 min | 3 min 59 s [M, 2026-10-13] |
| `prep_goldens` | ~5 min | 7 x ~45 s; prep_ref took 42 s [M] |
| Image pull | [U] | runs in parallel with prep |
| ETX gates and round invocations | ~4 min | 23 invocations x ~10 s |
| vLLM | ~20-35 min | 7-12 processes x ~3 min [E]: load, compile, warm-up, 4 x 5 samples |
| Routing | ~2 min | |
| **Total** | **~40-60 min** | about $2 at $1.99/h |

Runner cap: 5400 s.

## 7. Qwen (next, same protocol; not in this session)

The Qwen path already carries multiple requests (`ETX_VL_REQUESTS`), batched dense and MoE tiles (`ETX_VL_BATCH`, NB
columns per weight read) and per-request goldens (`--golden<r>`). Its kept build uses two features that are batch-1
only: the narrow router and the folded top-k. The Qwen batch arms therefore start from the kept build without those
two features. Qwen's staging (61 GB, builds) is the joint box's largest unmeasured cost, so its batch study is
prepared after this one and reuses this rule module (`etx/session/batch.py`) unchanged.
