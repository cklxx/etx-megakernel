# Per-phase budget, 2026-10-01

What each phase of one layer costs, against what its own bytes and width say it should cost. Everything here is
computed from data already on disk; no new runs. The point is to let the residuals pick the next target, after four
mechanisms proposed today were refuted by our own measurements.

Build: switch set B (XCD, CHAIN, DSL_GEMV, DSL_TOPK, DSL_ATTN=head, FOLD, TOUCH, COH) at `ETX_VL_CU=256`, on the
MI300X VF box (ROCm 7.14). Step 5.285 ms/token, 32/32 tokens + CHECK-DSL OK. Trace:
`results_2026-10-01/round4/trace_r4_ctrl.log` (total span 5294.1 us).

> **Correction (round 11, 2026-10-01).** Every per-phase number below was derived from a trace taken with
> `--max-steps 8`, which stops in the *prompt* phase where `seq_len <= 8`. The weight-streaming phases are
> unaffected (they do not depend on sequence length), but `attn` was measured at 1.91 us/layer when its decode
> value is 6.32 — understated by 0.21 ms/token — and the "4.3x partition imbalance" reported for it is an
> artefact of partitions 1-4 hitting the kernel's `partition_start_token_idx >= seq_len` early exit. There is
> no imbalance in the decode regime (all 20 tasks 5.72-6.68 us). See results_2026-10-01/round11/FINDING.md.
> Future traces must run without `--max-steps` so they land on a decode step.


## What the trace columns are

From `etx/codegen/kernel.py` (timestamp sites) and `etx/runtime/include/etx/host.h:326-343` (the dump):

| column | definition | what it bundles |
|---|---|---|
| `wait` | mean of `tr[1] - tr[0]` | event poll **+ acquire + ctx/prologue setup + one barrier**, from task entry to body start |
| `body` | mean of `tr[2] - tr[1]` | the tile body **+ the all-wave `ETX_RELEASE_DOMAIN` drain + one barrier** |
| `first-ready` | `min(tr[1])` relative to the step start | when the phase's first task begins its body |
| `last-ready` | `max(tr[1]) - min(tr[1])` | spread of body-start times across the phase's tasks (workgroup start skew) |
| `last-done` | `max(tr[2]) - min(tr[1])` | first body start to last body+drain end |

Two things the columns **cannot** do. (1) `wait` cannot be attributed to the event protocol: it also holds the
acquire, the argument/ctx setup and a barrier, and for a phase that is simply not due yet it is mostly legitimate
idling (o_proj waits 23.6 us while the attention block runs — that is not a cost). (2) The **arrive is in neither
column**; it lands in the gap between one phase's `last-done` and the next phase's `first-ready`. Phase durations
below are therefore taken as `delta` = difference of consecutive `first-ready`, which does include it.

Measured values are the mean over layers 1..46 (layer 0 carries prologue warm-up, layer 47 precedes `lm_head`).

## The achievable rate

From `results_2026-10-01/hw_stream.log` on this box, `contig` mapping (our `dsl_gemv_rows` gives each task a
contiguous `[r0, r1)` row range, so `contig` is the right comparison; `rows` is headroom we do not currently take).
Per-workgroup rate = bytes per workgroup / (phase span − 3.0 us floor):

| source row | width | B/workgroup | span us | per-WG rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gate_up x192` | 192 | 319488 | 19.03 | 19931 B/us |
| `gate_up` | 296 | 319488 | 38.21 | 9074 B/us |
| `o_proj x148` | 148 | 28672 | 4.21 | 23696 B/us |
| `o_proj` | 296 | 28672 | 7.14 | 6926 B/us |

Interpolating each pair linearly in width to 256 gives 13249 and 11458 B/us; I use the mean, **12354 B/us at width
256** (±7%, the spread of the two estimates). `moe_up` runs at width 192 and uses 19931 B/us directly.

Caveats, all of which make this an estimate rather than a measurement: `hw_bench` uses 256-thread workgroups while
ETX uses 1024; its `grid_barrier` executes `buffer_inv sc1` every pass, so its numbers are cold-start (this matches
the pre-COH megakernel and does not bias weight phases, since 6.08 GB/token is never cache-resident, but it does
understate what a COH phase could reach on cached data); and the width-256 rate is interpolated, not measured.

- **qkv's 5.35 us residual is the folded `in_norm`, not waste.** `qkv_0` is unfolded (body 6.68 us) and `qkv_1..47`
  are folded (11.98), so the fold costs 5.30 us inside qkv — against a separate `in_norm` phase that measured 3.31 us
  of body plus a hand-off edge. Charged that way qkv streams at 99 % of achievable. Nothing to fix here.
- **attn is at or above rate.** 105 KB/task in 1.91 us = 55 KB/us per workgroup, *above* the HBM per-workgroup rate.
  The KV cache is 2.10 MB/layer x 48 = 100 MB, which fits the 256 MB Infinity Cache, so attn reads at MALL speed
  (11.9 TB/s measured, `chipsandcheese`; our `hw_restart.log` warm row gives 64 KB/us per workgroup for L2-resident
  data). Nothing to fix here.
- **prep and reduce are latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound.** prep moves 3.0 KB/task in a 6.06 us body = 0.5 KB/us,
  4 % of a workgroup's streaming rate; reduce 20.8 KB in 5.57 us = 3.7 KB/us, 19 %. Both are dependent chains of
  memory round trips, so the streaming table does not apply and the whole body is charged as residual.
- **router is at the phase floor.** 2 KB/task cannot fill 3.48 us; `hw_stream`'s 896 B row at 296 workgroups is
  3.21 us, i.e. the floor itself. The only lever is fewer tasks, and the CU sweep (`results_2026-10-01/width/`)
  already measured the whole-model value of that at 0.067 ms.

## What to measure next, ranked by residual x cheapness of the deciding measurement

1. **The MoE GEMVs at 40–49 % of achievable — 1.23 ms/token, the largest item by a factor of two.** The same DSL
   tile family reaches 99 % on dense shapes, so this is specific to the MoE forms. Three candidates the trace cannot
   separate: (a) the expert gather — 8 scattered 6.29 MB expert blocks selected by top-k instead of one contiguous
   sweep; (b) the silu and routing-weight epilogue arithmetic competing with the load stream; (c) LDS staging plus
   the per-task top-k recompute. **Deciding measurement for (a): a `hw_bench stream` variant that reads 8 scattered
   blocks versus one contiguous block at the same total bytes and width** — a small addition to a benchmark we
   already have, ~20 s of GPU. If scattered ≈ contiguous, (a) is out and the cause is in our tile, which then needs
   a load-only build of the MoE tile to separate (b) from (c).
2. **prep + reduce, 0.56 ms/token, latency-bound.** **Deciding measurement: `hw_bench lat` on this box** — the mode
   exists, the binary is already built at `~/sess/hw_bench` there, and it has never been run on this machine. Dividing
   prep's 6.06 us body by the measured dependent round trip gives the chain length: ~5 hops means it is near its floor
   and only retiling (fusing prep into attn, as `dsl_attn` in `model.py:171` already sketches) can help; ~15 hops means
   there is ordering to fix inside the tile. ~20 s, no code change.
3. **In-phase skew, 0.76 ms/token**, concentrated in qkv (2.77 us) and router (4.19 us). **No new run needed:**
   compare the trace's spread against `hw_stream`'s span−median at matched width, both already on disk. Expect most
   of it to be hardware workgroup dispatch and therefore not addressable; confirm before spending anything on it.
4. **Hand-off, 0.449 ms/token, 1.17 us per edge.** Near the measured floor of the event protocol. The lever is fewer
   edges (fusing phases), not a faster protocol; `dsl_attn` would remove one. No measurement needed to decide.
5. **router, 0.16 ms/token**, already at the phase floor; the CU sweep bounded the whole-model gain at 0.067 ms. Low
   value, no measurement needed.

Missing data this document could not supply: `hw_bench lat` on this box (item 2), and any measurement separating the
MoE tile's arithmetic from its access pattern (item 1) — neither exists yet, both are cheap on a box we still have.

# Corrections and the skew verdict (round 10, same day)

The table above was computed from a **pre-round-7 build** (5.285 ms/token, top-k folded into the expert tasks).
Round 7 split that fold (`ETX_VL_FOLD_TOPK=0`, −0.231 ms) and round 8 measured the current build at **5.057
ms/token**. What follows restates the totals from `results_2026-10-01/round8/trace_r8base.bin` (the current build,
60362 tasks, 9 phases per layer since `topk` is a phase again), and settles item 3, the in-phase skew.

## Corrections to the table above

| | above | now | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `moe_up` of achievable | 49 % | **65 %** | round 8; the per-task top-k was inside its body |
| `moe_down` of achievable | 40 % | **66 %** | round 8, same reason |
| run-to-run spread | ±0.020 ms | **±0.003 ms** within one binary | round 8, 3 runs per build; the larger figure was *across* builds |
| `router` in-phase skew | 4.34 µs/layer | **0.68 µs/layer** | the method overstated it, see below |

## The totals, restated for the 5.057 build

Layer period 100.71 µs (mean of layers 1..45, consecutive `first-ready` deltas), 48 layers = 4.834 ms; `lm_head`,
`argmax` and the prologue make up the remaining 0.223 ms.

| | µs/layer | ms/token | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean task bodies | 74.95 | 3.598 | 71 % |
| work imbalance inside a phase (critical body − mean body) | 8.55 | 0.410 | 8 % |
| start delay of the phase's critical task | 7.96 | 0.382 | 8 % |
| hand-off between phases (9 edges) | 9.25 | 0.444 | 9 % |
| lm_head + argmax + prologue | — | 0.223 | 4 % |

The middle two rows are the 0.760 ms the first half of this document called "in-phase skew and tail". Splitting
them needed a different statistic: a phase ends when its **last-finishing** task ends, so

```
phase span = (that task's start delay) + (that task's body)
```

and `span − mean body`, which the table above used, silently charges the *difference between the mean and the
critical body* — i.e. work imbalance — to skew. That is what inflated `router`: 97 of its 256 tasks are empty
(M = 128 rows over 256 tasks at `ETX_VL_CU=256`, so half the tasks own no row and return), which halves the mean
and leaves 4.34 µs looking like skew where the real start delay is 0.68 µs.

## Verdict: the start delay is hardware, the imbalance is ours

**Start delay, 0.382 ms/token — not ours.** Against `hw_stream` on this box, which measures the same quantity
(`span − WG median`) with no events, no ETX and the same hardware dispatch:

| phase | tasks × B | ETX start delay | `hw_stream` row | its span − median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| o_proj | 256 × 64 K | 0.43 | `o_proj` 296 × 28 K | 0.62 |
| qkv | 256 × 80 K | 2.66 | `q/kv_a` 296 × 51 K | 2.90 |
| moe_up | 192 × 256 K | 0.51 | `gate_up x192` 192 × 312 K | 2.63 |
| moe_down | 256 × 96 K | 1.71 | `down` 296 × 156 K | 10.41 |
| router | 256 × 2 K | 0.68 | `router` 296 × 896 B | 0.29 |

ETX is at or below the hardware's own spread everywhere except `router`, where it is 0.39 µs/layer worse
(0.019 ms/token). Nothing here is worth spending on: the queue order, the arrival order and the last-arriver flush
together cost less than the dispatcher's own jitter.

**Work imbalance, 0.410 ms/token — ours, and it is almost entirely `attn`.** Per-task bodies of `attn_11`:

```
coord 0,5,10,15   body 5.16 5.32 5.24 5.36 us     <- partition 0 of each of the 4 kv heads
the other 16      body 1.08 .. 1.44 us
```

One partition in five does **4.3×** the work of the other four, so the phase costs 6.04 µs where an even split of
the same 10 µs of work over 5 partitions would cost about 2.4. That is **≈3.6 µs/layer = 0.17 ms/token**, the
largest addressable item left after the MoE residual, and it is a work-division question (how the imported
`attn_mfma16_g8`'s partitions divide the 1024-token context, `parts = ceil(MAXLEN/PART) = 5` at
`examples/vllm_llm/model.py:55,58`), not a placement or protocol one — round 4 already showed placement changes
nothing here.

Cheapest deciding measurement: none needed to see the imbalance, it is in the trace. One ~20 s run would confirm a
fix: rebuild with a different `PART` (so the partition count and per-partition span change) and re-read the
`attn` bodies. If partition 0 stays long regardless of `PART`, the split is inside the imported kernel and the
lever is the partition count, not the size.

The rest of the imbalance, 0.24 ms/token, is spread across `router` (empty tasks, harmless — they cost 0.4 µs and
the phase is bounded by the working ones) and `moe_up`/`moe_down` (1.04 and 0.00 µs/layer), none of it worth a
change.
