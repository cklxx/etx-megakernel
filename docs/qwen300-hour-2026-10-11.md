A (15 minutes) checks the kept, stamps and host-stage builds afresh at positions
**0 and 1040**, exhaustively through `host --check 48`. Each gets its own
identity-bound certificate archive and full **32/32** free-running HF gate with
CHECK-DSL before its measurements. No historical certificate is imported.
Failure of one diagnostic build does not hide the other probes. The last
15 seconds of A are reserved for the stop decision.

A1/A2 take a diagnostic snapshot at **decode position 1024**, outside the step
wall timer, then finish the whole 32-token run. A deadline-bounded CPU reader
writes `A_edges.json`, preserving every raw word and task, event dependencies,
producer membership, arrival owner and both gap endpoints. Each consumer/event
dependency has B-minus-arrival and poll-minus-arrival bounds. The effective
latest-readiness edge of each critical consumer is selected once; tied fan-in
edges are not added twice. All-edge and critical-projection distributions are
separate. Thresholds apply to the latter, not the sum of parallel grid edges.

Busy means the previous task's measured T..E or its owned final-arrival after
stamp covers the entire producer arrival bracket. The latter proves it is
still in its arrival path after E. Free means there is no previous task, or the consumer has
already been taken before the bracket. The interval from previous E to consumer
T is **ambiguous**: releases, arrives, dequeue and idle cannot be separated by
these stamps when no later owned arrival proves it busy. Nonfinal producer arrivals are **unobserved**. `all_producers`
retains all logical producer arcs compactly; the reader does not invent their
individual arrival stamps. This is deliberately conservative
(`docs/INBODY-INSTRUMENT-2026-10-11.md:25-45`). The threshold price uses the
**legacy critical B-D gap**, on the same basis as the old 0.280/0.145 pools and
the 1.00-us floor. Arrival stamps improve classification, not the price boundary.
The separate B-A bounds have a different boundary and are also retained.
Publication remains inside the legacy gap price; it is not subtracted as a saving or added twice
(`docs/INBODY-INSTRUMENT-2026-10-11.md:70-75`).

S1's G is separate from the A1 legacy 1.00-us pool classification. It sums
stamped **P10 arrive**, **P5/P11 observation**, and combined **P4/P6/P7 setup**
intervals, clipped to each critical gap so parallel/body time is not counted
twice. The two booked attention boundaries are excluded. Observation subtracts
**0.951 us**, the conservative end of the registered idle-price range; arrive
and combined setup use zero where no isolated idle price exists. Each class
must contribute **>=0.625 us/layer** before it counts in G. P1 fetch, isolated
P7 barrier and nonfinal producer arrivals are unobserved, with no invented
credit (`docs/PLAN-300TOKS-2026-10-11.md:301`). The raw atom prices remain in the
output even when a class does not qualify; the A1 below-floor count and measured
minimum remain in the pool output.

A2 sums **E-B** of selected critical attention/reduce tasks for each of all 48
layers, including the wrapper/drain/barrier body. It reports layer prices and
the candidate body that would need to beat them by the registered cut. The
**2.92+2.81=5.73 us/layer serial floor is an estimate**, not an attainable
replacement (`docs/FLOORS-2026-10-08.md:241-242`). Positive headroom is only a
necessary condition. No Job 1/vendor dispatch span is used
(`docs/CITATION-AUDIT-2026-10-11.md:234-240`).

A3/A4 use an independent **ETX_HOUR_PROFILE** host build, with stamps off.
For all 32 decode steps they record parameter upload, reset submission,
residency, launch, wait, retirement/readback, instrumentation remainder,
queued-reset device interval and kernel device envelope. Token device spans
are read after the full run. Kernel execution is subtracted from wait once;
execution outside the token timers is named separately. This accounts for
wall-minus-token-device time without pretending it is all CPU work. Reset
events bracket the queued operations and can include submission stalls; this
is not pure reset-kernel instruction time. The kept build uses host reset:
there is **no epoch mechanism** to price, and an unexpected device reset tail
fails closed rather than being assigned zero.

B (22 minutes) screens C1 and C5 against KEY32-on kept in three alternating
adjacent pairs each, with fresh imported-launch/DSL gates and 32/32 on **every**
sample. Flags are default off. Native spill costs are retained; static wrapper
instruction counts confer no timing credit. Their ceilings share the router
body allowance (`docs/ISA-FIXES-2026-10-11.md:19`).

S2 runs after those pairs and before any C/D work. The existing attention
report has no measured legal replacement (`attainable_cut_ms=null`), so R2 is
**zero**, not its estimated ceiling. H sums attributable named host time,
capped by the **0.086 ms** remainder: upload, residency, launch, retirement/
readback, wait minus kernel execution and queued reset device time, and reset
submission beyond its booked **0.049 ms**. The booked reset portion, device
execution outside token timers and instrumentation overhead are excluded.
Host pursuit classifications do not replace the S2 sum.
The stop row prints G, R2, H and their total; a body KEEP does not bypass S2
(`docs/PLAN-300TOKS-2026-10-11.md:306-308`).

C (27 minutes) runs **NO_TOUCH**. **ISOLATE_POLLER** is declared in the
manifest with `enabled: false` and a reason reserving it for a later session.
Both retain their switches and thresholds; neither is a new fusion prototype.
An enabled body arm uses the existing fresh exhaustive import checks at positions
0/1040, CHECK-DSL and one complete 32/32 token gate, followed by **three adjacent
pinned wall pairs** in alternating order (kept/candidate, candidate/kept,
kept/candidate). Every timing sample must itself pass 32/32. Partial pair
populations produce NOT-COMPLETE with no aggregate gain. Each output row carries
its registered threshold, including when the arm fails or is never run.

## Completion and limitations

Every subprocess has a deadline within its tranche and the aggregate cap.
Progress is line buffered every step and every five seconds while waiting.
The report-enabled fused host has a five-second HIP watchdog that dumps events;
the runner probes ps/rocm-smi and kills a stuck step after ten seconds without
progress, including the first step. Longer initialization silence is probed
after expected time plus 180 seconds, and at any deadline. Docker has its own
remaining-budget timeout and explicit cleanup, because killing its client alone
does not stop its container. No retry overwrites a log.

All command outputs are full and unfiltered in the runner-selected directory.
Certificates, compiler logs/seals, configuration, manifest, binary stamps,
per-edge/phase reports and round populations are kept there. `report.json` and
the final printed table cover **every registered threshold**, including missing
or NOT-RUN rows. A partial arm is NOT-COMPLETE; later independent arms continue
within their own remaining deadlines. No shortened population yields an
aggregate verdict or target number.

Three limits prevent a stronger promise. First, two timestamps cannot classify
every individual producer arrival or previous-task exit exactly; ambiguous
edges may leave pool labels unresolved; only missing G or S1 decides continuation. Second, ON stamps allocate,
store and spill differently: the report retains ON, null-stamp and kept/OFF
controls but does **not** subtract away perturbation or transfer ON pools to OFF
as exclusive savings. Third, fresh checks and cold loading can exhaust 15
minutes, and 20 cold D invocations may exceed ten minutes. There is no guarantee
of a complete final population within the cap; excluding every unprepared C
arm gives D 30 minutes, but the deadline still wins. Persistent multi-build serving or an exact
previous-task exit stamp would require additional CPU work outside this brief.
The job is runnable with its staging preconditions; these limitations are
reported as NOT-COMPLETE/UNRESOLVED, never concealed by shorter runs.

Remaining differences from the broader revised plan are outside this stop/cap
change: A still uses a single snapshot/repetition rather than the unchanged
five-repeat in-body job and its OFF/ON perturbation validity gates (lines
297-299); the row-10 **80%** close rule (line 300) is absent, and fetch and
isolated barrier atoms are not stamped. X8 still runs in A
on the independent profile build, B still screens C1/C5 rather than X8/X5, and
there is no X5 candidate price (lines 304-307), hence R2=0. A2's **0.142 ms**
headroom classification and A4's strict **>0.030 ms** reset classification are
retained; the new plan uses X5's **<=7.69 us/layer** candidate basis and X8's
**>=0.030 ms** host threshold (lines 306-307). C now adds NO_TOUCH with
independent intra-body stamps and declares deferred polling isolation,
but retains optional prototype screening and has no S3 **0.847 ms**
target-attempt gate (lines 310-316); its existing all-ineligible skip still
transfers 27 minutes to D. These differences
were not silently replaced with new experiments. All line references in this
paragraph are to `docs/PLAN-300TOKS-2026-10-11.md` section 7.1 at the pinned
`main` revision above.

Prior CPU gate evidence is recorded in `docs/evidence/qwen300-hour-cpu-2026-10-11.json`
and full local logs under `results_qwen300_hour_cpu_20261011/`. No GPU correctness,
wall gain, removable-time certificate or 300 tok/s success was measured here.
